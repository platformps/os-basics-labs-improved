About this lab

A phone goes everywhere with you, so if it is lost or stolen, a passcode is what keeps a stranger out. In this lab you set a PIN or passcode, use it, fail it on purpose to see the lockout, set up fingerprint and face unlock, and then remove all the locks.

Choose the phone with the Phone buttons at the top of the simulation. Physical actions, such as pressing the side button, touching the fingerprint sensor, or looking at the phone, are buttons under the phone. The simulation cannot scan a real finger or face; it walks you through the same choices and rules. Use 123456 as the PIN and passcode. Never use a code that simple on your own phone.

To practice on your own phone with TalkBack or VoiceOver, use the screen reader edition of this lab, listed on the hub. Choose a code you will remember, and do the clean-up steps at the end.

Part 1: Passcode lock on Android

Step 1: Set a PIN

  1. Choose the Android phone. Open Settings, then Lock screen and AOD, then Screen lock type. (Names vary: Biometrics and security, Lock screen, or Security on other phones.)

Which unlocking methods does the phone offer?

Swipe, Pattern, PIN, Password, and None, plus fingerprints and face recognition as biometric options. Real phones vary by model and Android version.

  1. Choose PIN. Enter 123456, choose Continue, enter it again to confirm, and choose Continue.
  2. Open Secure lock settings (enter the PIN when asked) and check that Lock instantly with side key is on.

Step 2: Use the PIN to unlock the screen

  1. Use Press the side (power) button to lock the phone. The screen goes dark.
  2. Press the side button again to wake the phone. Enter 123456 and choose OK.

What happens?

The phone unlocks and shows the screen that was open before it was locked.

  1. Lock and wake the phone again. Enter 654321, which is the wrong PIN.

What happens?

The screen stays locked and the phone says the PIN is incorrect.

  1. Enter the wrong PIN a few more times.

What happens?

After 5 wrong tries, the phone makes you wait 30 seconds before you can try again. Many wrong guesses in a row suggest someone is trying to guess the PIN, so the wait slows them down.

  1. Wait 30 seconds (or use Let 30 seconds pass), then enter the correct PIN.

Step 3: Fingerprint unlock

  1. Open Settings, Security and privacy, Fingerprints. Enter the PIN when asked. The PIN stays as a backup in case the sensor fails, and after every restart.
  2. Choose Register fingerprint, then use Touch the fingerprint sensor until registration reaches 100 percent.
  3. Lock the phone, wake it, and use Touch the fingerprint sensor to unlock it.
  4. Optional: use Restart the phone, then try the fingerprint again. Notice that Android asks for the PIN first.

Step 4: Face recognition

  1. Open Security and privacy, Face recognition, and enter the PIN.
  2. Choose Register face, then use Hold the phone in front of your face.
  3. Lock the phone, wake it, and use Hold the phone in front of your face to unlock it.

Step 5: Clean up

  1. In Fingerprints, remove Fingerprint 1. In Face recognition, choose Remove face data.
  2. Go to Lock screen and AOD, Screen lock type.

What happens? Why?

The phone asks for the PIN, so someone who picks up an unlocked phone cannot change or remove the lock.

  1. Enter the PIN and choose None.
  2. Lock the phone and wake it again.

What happens?

The phone opens without asking for anything, and Screen lock type no longer asks for a PIN either.

What other security settings does the phone offer?

Answers vary. In the simulation: auto lock when the screen turns off, auto factory reset after 15 failed attempts, and lock network and security.

Part 2: Passcode lock on iOS

Step 1: Turn on a passcode

  1. Choose the iPhone. Open Settings, then Face ID and Passcode (Touch ID and Passcode, or just Passcode, on other models).
  2. Choose Turn Passcode On. Enter 123456, then enter it again. (Passcode Options offers 4-digit, custom numeric, and alphanumeric codes.)
  3. Notice that Require Passcode is set to Immediately.

Step 2: Use the passcode

  1. Press the side button to lock the iPhone, and press it again to wake it. Choose Swipe up to unlock and enter 123456. A 6-digit passcode is checked as soon as you type the sixth digit; there is no OK key.

What happens?

The iPhone unlocks.

  1. Lock and wake the iPhone, then enter 654321.

What happens?

The iPhone vibrates, says the passcode is wrong, and stays locked.

  1. Enter the wrong passcode 5 more times.

What happens?

After the sixth wrong try the iPhone shows "iPhone Unavailable, try again in 1 minute." Each further mistake makes the wait longer.

  1. Wait 1 minute (or use Let 1 minute pass), then enter the correct passcode.

Step 3: Face ID

iPhones with a Home button use Touch ID (fingerprint) instead; the steps are similar: Touch ID and Passcode, Add a Fingerprint.

  1. In Settings, Face ID and Passcode (enter the passcode), choose Set Up Face ID, then Get Started. If moving your head is hard, Accessibility Options offers Use Partial Circle.
  2. Use Move your head in a circle for the first scan, choose Continue, do the second scan, and choose Done.
  3. Lock and wake the iPhone. Use Look at the iPhone. When the lock opens, choose Swipe up to open.

What does Require Attention for Face ID do, and why might a VoiceOver user see it turned off?

It makes Face ID check that your eyes are open and looking at the screen. When VoiceOver is set up, iOS turns it off by default, because blind users may not look directly at the screen. It is a little less secure without it.

Step 4: Clean up

  1. Open Settings, Face ID and Passcode.

What happens? Why?

The iPhone asks for the passcode first, so someone else cannot change or remove it.

  1. Choose Reset Face ID, then Turn Passcode Off and confirm.
  2. Lock and wake the iPhone, then swipe up.

What happens?

The iPhone opens to whatever was on screen before, without a passcode.

Corrections from the original lab

  • "Slide to unlock" was removed in iOS 10, the minimum version the lab lists. Current iPhones use Swipe up.
  • "iPhone is Disabled" is now "iPhone Unavailable."
  • The iOS steps say "Touch OK" after the passcode. A 6-digit passcode has no OK key; it is checked after the sixth digit.
  • The last iOS answer says the Settings screen appears. The iPhone shows whatever was open before it was locked.
  • Samsung now calls the power key the side key, and its security settings are under Lock screen and AOD, and Security and privacy.
  • Question 11 is added about Require Attention for Face ID, which matters for screen reader users.
  • The original's seven screenshots have no text descriptions.
