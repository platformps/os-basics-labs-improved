About this lab

In this lab you control screen auto-rotation, brightness, and location services (GPS) on an Android phone and an iPhone.

Choose the phone with the Phone buttons at the top of the simulation. Things you do with your hands on a real phone, such as turning it sideways or swiping down from the top, are buttons under the phone, in the group Physical buttons and movements. The phone tells you what the screen does in response.

Using a screen reader on a real phone? TalkBack and VoiceOver announce "landscape" or "portrait" when the screen rotates. The screen reader edition of this lab, listed on the hub, has the steps.

Part 1: Auto-rotation

Auto-rotation switches the screen between portrait (upright) and landscape (sideways) to match how you hold the device.

Step 1: Auto-rotation on Android

  1. Choose the Android phone. Open the quick settings panel with Swipe down from the top. Check that Auto rotate is off. (You can also find it in Settings, Display, Auto rotate screen.)
  2. With auto rotate off, use Rotate the phone 90 degrees clockwise.

What happens to the screen?

Nothing: it stays in portrait.

  1. Rotate the phone back upright. Turn Auto rotate on.
  2. Rotate the phone 90 degrees clockwise again.

What happens to the screen?

It rotates to landscape to match the phone.

Step 2: Auto-rotation on iOS

  1. Choose the iPhone. Open Control Center with Swipe down from the top right corner. (On iPhones with a Home button, you swipe up from the bottom edge instead.)
  2. Turn Portrait Orientation Lock on. Its icon is a lock inside a circular arrow.
  3. Go Home, open Calculator, and rotate the iPhone 90 degrees clockwise.

What happens to the screen?

Nothing: the lock keeps it in portrait, so Calculator stays the basic calculator.

  1. Rotate the iPhone back upright. Open Control Center again and turn Portrait Orientation Lock off. Go back to Calculator.
  2. Rotate the iPhone 90 degrees clockwise.

What happens to the screen?

It rotates to landscape, and Calculator switches to the scientific calculator.

Part 2: Brightness

Step 1: Brightness on Android

  1. Open quick settings, or Settings, then Display. Move the Brightness slider all the way up (right). With a keyboard, use the arrow keys or End.

What happens?

The screen gets brighter (100 percent).

  1. In Settings, Display, turn Adaptive brightness on. Then use Change the room lighting and pick bright sunlight or a dark room.

What happens when adaptive brightness is on?

The phone uses its light sensor to set the brightness for you: brighter in sunlight, dimmer in the dark. It also learns from the changes you make.

  1. Turn adaptive brightness off and move the slider all the way down (left). Go Home.

What happens?

The screen gets darker (0 percent, the dimmest setting; it does not turn off).

Step 2: Brightness on iOS

  1. Open Control Center and change Display Brightness.
  2. Open Settings, Display and Brightness. Explore: Light and Dark appearance, Automatic and its Options (a custom schedule), Text Size, Bold Text, True Tone, Night Shift, Auto-Lock, Raise to Wake, and Display Zoom.

What else can you configure in Display and Brightness?

Night Shift (warmer colors after dark), Auto-Lock (how soon the iPhone locks itself), Raise to Wake (the screen wakes when you pick it up), and Display Zoom (larger text and controls), as well as appearance, text size, bold text, and True Tone.

Part 3: GPS and location services

Phones find their location with GPS satellites, and also with nearby Wi-Fi networks and cell towers. Apps ask for permission to use it.

Step 1: Location on Android

  1. In quick settings (or Settings, Location), turn Location off.
  2. Go Home and open Maps.

How does the phone know roughly where it is with location off?

Maps can estimate a general area from the Wi-Fi network and the internet address the phone is using, but not precisely.

  1. Choose Show my location (the crosshair).

What happens?

Because location is off, Android asks to turn it on. Choose Turn on, and Maps shows your location accurately.

Step 2: Location on iOS

  1. Open Settings, Privacy and Security, Location Services.

Which apps use Location Services, and when?

Answers vary on a real iPhone. In the simulation: Camera and Find My and Maps while using the app, Weather always, Safari Websites never.

  1. Choose Camera and set location access to Never.

What is geotagging, and why might you turn it off?

Geotagging stores the place a photo was taken inside the photo file. Anyone who gets the photo may see where you live, work, or are right now, so turning it off protects your privacy.

  1. Go back to Location Services and turn it off. Confirm with Turn Off.
  2. Go Home, open Maps, and choose Current Location. A message tells you Location Services is off; choose Settings.
  3. Turn Location Services on, go back to Maps, and choose Current Location again.

Was the iPhone able to locate you?

Yes, once Location Services is on and Maps has permission.

Corrections from the original lab

  • The answers for the iOS rotation questions are reversed in the original. With Portrait Orientation Lock on, the screen stays in portrait; with it off, the screen rotates. This version has them the right way around.
  • "Swipe down from the top right corner" opens Control Center only on iPhones with Face ID. On iPhones with a Home button you swipe up from the bottom edge.
  • The original asks for iOS 7.0 or later but uses names from iOS 16 and later, such as Privacy and Security.
  • On iOS, Auto-Brightness is in Accessibility settings, not Display and Brightness.
  • A geotagging step and question are added. The course's final exam asks about geotagging, which no course content covers.
  • Typo in the original: "ambient lightening conditions."
