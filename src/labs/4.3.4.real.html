About this edition

This edition is for doing the lab on your own phone with TalkBack (Android) or VoiceOver (iPhone). You only need one phone; do the part that matches it. You will physically turn the phone, so hold it securely. Your screen reader tells you what changed: both say "landscape" or "portrait" when the screen rotates.

Gestures used in this lab

  • Move between items: swipe right or left. Activate or flip a switch: double-tap.
  • Adjust a slider: TalkBack, swipe up or down, or use the volume keys while on it. VoiceOver, swipe up to increase or down to decrease.
  • Android quick settings: swipe down from the top edge with two fingers; do it twice to open all quick settings.
  • iPhone Control Center: touch the status bar at the top so VoiceOver reads it, then swipe up with three fingers. On iPhones with a Home button, the same works.

Part A: Android with TalkBack

Auto-rotation

  1. Open quick settings. Find Auto rotate (Samsung may call it Portrait when it is off). If it is on, double-tap to turn it off. You can also use Settings, Display, Auto rotate screen.
  2. Open any app that rotates, such as Chrome. Turn the phone 90 degrees clockwise and hold it there for a moment. Listen.

What happens to the screen with auto-rotate off?

Nothing; it stays in portrait, and TalkBack says nothing about rotation.

  1. Turn the phone back upright, turn Auto rotate on, and turn the phone 90 degrees clockwise again.

What happens now?

The screen rotates to landscape, and TalkBack announces "Landscape."

Brightness

  1. In quick settings, move to the Brightness slider and raise it all the way. (Or Settings, Display, Brightness.) If you have some vision, notice the change; otherwise TalkBack reads the percentage.
  2. In Settings, Display, turn Adaptive brightness on. Move between a bright window and a dark room and check the slider again.
  3. Turn adaptive brightness off and lower the slider all the way.

What does adaptive brightness do?

It uses the light sensor to set the brightness for you, brighter in bright places and dimmer in dark ones.

Tip: TalkBack users who never look at the screen can save battery and add privacy with a dark screen. TalkBack's own menu has a Hide screen (or Dim screen) option on many versions.

Location

  1. In quick settings, turn Location off (or Settings, Location, Use location).
  2. Open Maps and find the Your location button (the crosshair). Double-tap it.

What happens?

Maps asks to turn on device location. With location off it can only guess a general area from the network.

  1. Choose Turn on and let Maps find you.

Part B: iPhone with VoiceOver

Auto-rotation

  1. Open Control Center. Move to Portrait Orientation Lock and double-tap so VoiceOver says it is selected (on).
  2. Go Home, open Calculator, and turn the iPhone 90 degrees clockwise.

What happens with the lock on?

Nothing; the screen stays in portrait and Calculator stays basic.

  1. Turn the iPhone upright, turn Portrait Orientation Lock off in Control Center, return to Calculator, and turn the iPhone again.

What happens with the lock off?

VoiceOver announces landscape, and Calculator becomes the scientific calculator with many more keys.

Brightness

  1. In Control Center, adjust the Display Brightness slider.
  2. Open Settings, Display and Brightness, and explore: Light and Dark, Automatic and Options, Text Size, Bold Text, True Tone, Night Shift, Auto-Lock, Raise to Wake, and Display Zoom.

What else can you configure in Display and Brightness?

Night Shift, Auto-Lock, Raise to Wake, Display Zoom, appearance, text size, bold text, and True Tone.

Tip: VoiceOver's Screen Curtain (triple-tap with three fingers) turns the display off while VoiceOver keeps working, for privacy.

Location and geotagging

  1. Open Settings, Privacy and Security, Location Services. Swipe through the list of apps and their access.
  2. Double-tap Camera and choose Never if you do not want photos to record where they were taken (geotagging). You can set it back afterward.
  3. Go back and turn Location Services off; confirm with Turn Off.
  4. Open Maps and double-tap the Current Location button. Listen to the alert, then choose Settings.
  5. Turn Location Services on, return to Maps, and try Current Location again.

Which apps use your location, and what is geotagging?

Answers vary. Geotagging stores where a photo was taken in the photo file, which anyone you share it with may see.

Corrections from the original lab

  • The original's iOS rotation answers are reversed: with the lock on the screen stays put; with it off, it rotates.
  • A geotagging step is added, because the final exam asks about it.
  • Control Center gestures differ by model; VoiceOver's status bar and three-finger swipe works on all.
