    About this lab

    In this lab you place apps and widgets on an Android home screen and move them between screens, create and rename a folder, and install and uninstall an app.

    The simulated phone is a Google Pixel style Android phone. Every touch and hold or drag on a real phone is an Actions button here, next to each app, widget, or folder. TalkBack users on real phones use the same kind of Actions menu. Home screens have 5 rows and 4 columns.

    Prefer to practice on your own Android phone with TalkBack? Use the TalkBack edition of this lab, listed on the hub.

    Part 1: Apps and widgets

    Android phones differ by maker and version, so on a real phone check its manual if a step looks different.

    Step 1: Gain access to the device

    1. The simulated phone is already unlocked. On a real phone, unlock it with your PIN, password, or pattern.

    Step 2: Add app shortcuts to the home screen

    1. On the home screen, choose All apps. The All apps screen lists every installed app.
    2. Choose the Actions button for Calculator, then Add to Home screen. On a real phone you touch and hold the icon and drag it to an empty space.
    3. Choose a screen and a position, or leave First empty space, and choose Place here.

    Step 3: Add widgets to the home screen

    Widgets are parts of apps that show live information on the home screen, such as the time or today's step count. They usually take more than one space. Widgets come with installed apps; you add them to a screen, you do not install them separately.

    1. Go to the home screen and choose Home screen options. On a real phone you touch and hold an empty part of the screen.
    2. Choose Widgets. The list groups widgets by app.
    3. Choose a widget, such as Fit daily goals, pick a screen with room for it, and choose Place here.

    Step 4: Move apps between screens

    1. Choose the Actions button for any app on the home screen, then Move to another screen or position, and pick Home screen 2. Widgets move the same way. On a real phone you drag the icon to the edge of the screen and wait for the next screen to slide in.

    Step 5: Remove apps or widgets from the home screen

    1. Choose the Actions button for Drive, then Remove. On a real phone you drag it to Remove at the top of the screen.

    Remove only takes the shortcut off the home screen. Drive is still installed and still listed in All apps, so you can add it back the way you did in Step 2. Uninstalling comes in Part 3.

    Part 2: Managing folders

    Step 1: Create a folder

    1. Make sure the same home screen has at least two apps, for example Calculator and Photos.
    2. Choose the Actions button for Calculator, then Create folder with Photos. On a real phone you drag one icon on top of the other. The new folder holds both apps.

    Step 2: Rename a folder

    1. Open the folder.
    2. In Folder name, type ITE Apps Folder and choose Save name.
    3. Go Back to close the folder.

    Step 3: Remove apps from a folder

    1. Open the folder again. Choose the Actions button for Photos, then Move out of folder to the home screen.

    After Photos is moved out of the folder, what happens to the folder and to Calculator?

    The folder disappears, because a folder needs at least two apps. Calculator goes back on the home screen where the folder was.

    Part 3: Managing apps

    Step 1: Install apps

    1. Open Play Store.
    2. Search for LastPass, a password manager, and choose LastPass Password Manager to see its details.
    3. Choose Install. When it finishes, LastPass is listed in All apps.

    Step 2: Uninstall apps

    1. Go to the home screen and open All apps.
    2. Choose the Actions button for LastPass, then Uninstall. On a real phone you touch and hold the icon and choose Uninstall, or drag it to Uninstall.
    3. Confirm with OK. LastPass is removed from the phone.

    What is the difference between removing an app from the home screen and uninstalling it?

    Removing takes away only the shortcut; the app stays installed and is still in All apps. Uninstalling deletes the app and its data from the phone, and removes its shortcuts and widgets too.

    How to use this simulation

    The simulated phone is in the region headed Simulated phone. It works with a keyboard and with NVDA, JAWS, Narrator, and VoiceOver. Nothing is installed, and nothing changes on a real phone.

    • Touch becomes a button. Where a real phone uses a tap, the simulation has a button: press Enter or Space. When the screen changes, focus moves to the screen title, a level 3 heading.
    • Switches such as Wi-Fi are announced as switches that are on or off. Press Space to flip them.
    • Touch and hold or drag becomes an Actions button next to each app or widget, with choices such as Move, Create folder, Remove, and Uninstall. This mirrors the Actions menu that TalkBack and VoiceOver users use on real phones.
    • Back and Home buttons sit under the phone screen, like the navigation bar or gestures on a real phone.
    • Messages that a phone shows briefly, such as "Connected," are spoken for you and stay on screen below the phone content.
    • Your checklist marks itself as you complete each task. The phone starts fresh each time you open this page; your answers and checklist are saved in this browser.

    Corrections from the original lab

    • The original says widgets "can be installed directly from a screen." Widgets come with installed apps; you add them to a screen.
    • The original targets Android 7.0. On current Android, Remove and Uninstall appear in the touch and hold menu instead of only as drag targets, and Play Store no longer shows an App Permissions screen with Accept when you install. Apps ask for permissions when they need them.
    • The original's 21 screenshots have no text descriptions, and several steps can only be understood from the pictures. Every step here is written out.
    • Question 2 is added so learners compare Remove with Uninstall, which the original explains only in a note.
